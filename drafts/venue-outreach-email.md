# Venue outreach: email drafts

Written as Alex (first person). Fill in the [brackets]. Keep it short: these are meant to be read on a phone in 20 seconds.

## What makes these work (quick rules)
- **Short.** About 80–150 words. Owners skim. One idea and one ask.
- **Their benefit first.** Not "I'd love to teach", but "a full table of paying guests on a quiet night".
- **One personal line** that proves you've actually been there ("your back room with the long tables", "your Sunday crowd"). Personalized emails get noticeably more replies.
- **One clear, easy ask.** "Could I stop by Tuesday afternoon for 10 minutes?" beats "let me know your thoughts".
- **Proof.** A link to the workshops page (photos of real workshops) does more than any adjective.
- **Subject line:** specific, ~5–8 words, mentions their venue.
- **Follow up.** Most replies come after a nudge. Follow up twice, each time with something new, then stop.
- **Better yet: walk in.** For cafes and bars, show up on a slow afternoon, buy a drink, ask for the owner/manager, and leave a card. Then send the email the same day ("great to meet you today...").

## Selling points (pick 2–3, don't list them all)
- Guaranteed spend: 2-drink minimum × ~[10–15] guests = [20–30] drinks on a slow night
- **Built-in second visit:** guests come back ~2 weeks later to pick up their fired piece (and usually buy something)
- New customers: I promote each event on Luma + Instagram, tagging the venue
- Zero work for staff: I bring everything, set up, and leave the tables clean
- Only needs: a few tables for ~2 hours, plus a shelf or box behind the counter for pickups
- Good content for their Instagram (people love posting their pieces)

## Email 1: first contact (cold)

**Subject options**
- Ceramic painting night at [Venue]?
- A sold-out table at [Venue] on a [Tuesday]
- Workshop idea for [Venue]

> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm Alex, a ceramic artist here in [neighborhood]. I'm a regular at [Venue], and [personal detail: e.g. your back room with the long tables would be perfect for this].
>
> I run 90-minute ceramic painting workshops: I make the pieces by hand, guests paint them over drinks, and I fire them so people can pick them up at the venue two weeks later. That means everyone orders at least two drinks during the workshop and comes back again for pickup.
>
> I bring all the supplies, promote the event, and clean up. All I'd need is a few tables on a quieter evening.
>
> Here's what past workshops look like: alexceramics.art/workshops
>
> Could I stop by [day] afternoon for 10 minutes to show you some pieces?
>
> Thanks!
> Alex
> [phone] · @ceramics.alex

*(~130 words)*

I host small ceramic painting workshops: guests paint my handmade pieces over drinks (2-drink min), and come back to pick them up fired 2 weeks later. Would you be open to hosting one on a quiet evening? Photos here: alexceramics.art/workshops

## Things to decide before sending
- [ ] Ideal group size and which nights/times to offer
- [ ] What the venue gets beyond drinks (nothing? a free first workshop for staff? a cut of tickets?)
- [ ] Where pickups live at the venue and for how long
- [ ] Workshops page live, so the link works
- [ ] A simple tracker: venue, contact, date sent, follow-ups, status (a notes app or spreadsheet is fine)
