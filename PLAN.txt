STORE: HOW IT WORKS
  Stripe is the "admin panel". The site reads the items from Stripe.

  Adding an item (Kilian, ~5 min each, Stripe Dashboard):
    1. Product catalog -> Add product: name, description, photo, one-time price
    2. Create a Payment Link for it:
         - Limit the number of payments: 1   (one-of-a-kind piece)
         - Collect shipping address (US only) + add the flat shipping rate
         - After payment: redirect to https://alexceramics.art/store.html?purchased=1
    3. Done. The item shows up on the site within ~5 minutes.
  Shortcut for step 2 (local only, gitignored): add products in the Dashboard, then
    node tools/stripe-links.mjs          (dry run)
    node tools/stripe-links.mjs --yes    (creates links with all the settings above)
    Needs a key in .env with Payment Links: Write. Relist a refunded piece with --product prod_...
       Optional: product metadata "order" = 1, 2, 3... to control the order on the page.
  When it sells, Stripe turns the link off automatically and the site shows it as "Sold".
  To take an item down without selling it: deactivate its Payment Link.

  Under the hood:
    A Netlify Function (/api/products) uses a read-only Stripe key to list the
    Payment Links and their products, and returns JSON to store.html. Results are
    cached for 5 minutes. The secret key never reaches the browser.
    Photos come from Stripe, resized via Netlify's image CDN.
    No monthly cost. Netlify's free tier covers far more traffic than we'll get.

  Fees: Stripe takes 2.9% + 30c per card sale (~$1.60 on a $45 bowl).

  Refunds / cancelling an order (Stripe Dashboard -> Payments -> the sale -> Refund):
    - Full or partial (partial = e.g. a chipped piece). Lands on their card in 5-10 days.
    - "Cancel an order" = refund it before shipping. There's no separate order system.
    - Stripe keeps its fee on refunds (~$1-2). A chargeback/dispute costs $15.
    - To relist a refunded piece: make a NEW Payment Link for the product.

SHIPPING (US only to start; no local pickup, since coordinating it isn't worth it yet)
  The flow when something sells:
    1. Stripe emails you "Payment received" (the Stripe phone app pings too)
    2. Stripe Dashboard -> Payments -> open it -> buyer's shipping address
    3. Pack it: wrap the piece in bubble wrap, put it in a box with ~2in of padding,
       then put that box inside a bigger box (double-boxing; ceramics need it)
    4. Buy a label on Pirate Ship (free account, discounted USPS/UPS rates)
       -> print at home on plain paper, or at a FedEx/UPS store
    5. Drop at the post office, or book a free USPS pickup from home
    6. Send the buyer the tracking number (Pirate Ship can email it for you)

  Costs per order (estimates, check Pirate Ship's calculator with a real box):
    Label    ~$8-15  for a 2-4 lb box, USPS Ground Advantage, depends on distance
    Packing  ~$3-6   boxes + bubble wrap (cheaper bought in bulk)
  What to charge: Stripe Payment Links only allow FIXED shipping prices (no
    live carrier rates). Start with a flat $12 US shipping fee. If big pieces cost
    more to ship, make a second "$18 large item" rate and use it on those links.

  Tickets: Luma (free plan: 5% + card fee, ~$1.88 on a $20 ticket).
    Link out to the Luma event page. Don't embed its checkout, because Apple Pay
    doesn't work inside Luma's embeds.
    No Luma calendar embed in v1. Add it once the first venue is set.

  What makes people book (the page answers these):
    1. What is it? -> photos of real people at a table with drinks + finished pieces
    2. Can I do this? -> no experience needed, everything provided
    3. Quick facts: $45 / everything provided / weeknights from 6:30pm /
       2-item min at the venue (food or drink) / pick up fired in ~2 weeks
       (No session length on the page; people assume 1-2 hours.)
    4. One obvious "Book" button near the top and again at the bottom
    5. FAQ: bring friends? solo OK? what do I paint? pickup? refunds?
    6. Private events (birthdays, bachelorettes, teams): "email to book"
    7. Warm, first-person, slightly playful tone, the way Alex writes
